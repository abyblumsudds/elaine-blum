How I Can Help

Neurodiverse- & Queer-Affirming Therapy Services

Services

ADHD

  • Assessing for ADHD and/or autism
  • Strategies for task initiation, organization, prioritization
  • Psycho-education surrounding ADHD’s natural rhythms (e.g. “Let’s do all the things!” to “How about we do anything but the thing?”)
  • Unlearning ableist, neuronormative narratives (e.g. “Why can’t I just do the thing?” or “I should be able to just...”)
  • Letter of support for medication and/or accommodation

Autism

  • Assessing for autism and/or ADHD
  • Strategies for social engagement and/or lowering social anxiety
  • Interpersonal and communication skills
  • Strategies for communicating and holding big emotions
  • Unlearning ableist, neuronormative narratives (e.g. “I shouldn’t be so upset about this.” or “I shouldn’t need so much recovery/alone time.”)
  • Finding routines that help prevent and/or lessen autistic burnout
  • Letters of support for medication and/or accommodation

Anxiety & Depression

  • Diversifying claming strategies
  • Strengthening tools for problem-solving
  • Empower and elevate sense of self-worth and confidence
  • Building sustainable routines
  • Finding language for untangling and unsticking stuck emotions
  • Empowering self-advocacy and social connectedness

Gender-Affirming Care

  • Letters of support for surgery (facial feminization, vaginoplasty, phalloplasty, mastectomy, hysterectomy) and/or HRT
  • Support in exploring, questioning, and clarifying concepts of sexuality, gender, orother identities
  • Support in transitioning (socially, medically, etc.)
  • Navigating relationships with family and friends whose values do not align with and/or support your own.
  • Unlearning patriarchal, white-supremacist, transphobic, and other oppressive standards; re-centering what you value.

Academic Stress (College, Grad School)

  • Questioning/clarifying life goals, values, and/or direction post-graduation
  • Support for high-demands culture of academia
  • Balancing work/life demands
  • Support for grad school and/or job market applications
  • Lessening tendencies toward perfectionism (and academia’s structural encouragement of such tendencies)

Trauma

  • Processing religious, sexual, racial, medical, combat, academic, and/or other trauma.
  • Processing through constructing a trauma narrative
  • Reprocessing utilizing EMDR.
  • Challenging internalized shame and/or oppressive narratives

What would sessions with me be like?

This depends on what best fits you! I like to tailor my approach to what you find edifying and practicable. I eclectically meld parts work, narrative therapy, queer/trans theory, feminist theory, EMDR, and more. My goal is to be a resource for you, to help you celebrate the strengths and tools already within you, and to form a collaborative relationship to buildupon them with you.

I want to help you build confidence in your own voice! Bring all of you!

Approaches

Neurodiverse-Affirming

This approach focuses on the idea that brains, minds, and individuals are built diversely, each with differing challenges and strengths. This means that individuals have diverse needs in support and communication as well. The approach must adapt to the needs of the specific brain! Whereas many mainstream models see certain neurotypes as defined by deficits that need to be “fixed,” I focus on finding the models and approaches that fit your brain and optimize its strengths.

Read more about why a neurodivergent-affirming approach matters here.

Queer & Trans Theory

Yes, this broadly includes all identities and experiences underthe LGBTQIA2S+ umbrella (gay, lesbian, bisexual, trans, intersex, asexual, two spirit, non-binary, and so on). It also acknowledges a commitment to critically engaging assumptions, expectations, and models that we take to be “normal.” Ideas of what is normal encourage - and coerce - conformity to that ideal. A queer approach looks for opportunities to stretch, to redefine, and/or to queer understandings of what must be the case to more inclusively conceive of what is livable. Conceived in this way, my queer/trans approach critically depends on affirms approaches that are anti-ableist, anti-racist, anti-classist, and so forth.

EMDR (Eye Movement Desensitization and Reprocessing)

A more somatic approach (focused on connecting emotions to sensations in the body) that is often used for reprocessing traumatic events. It utilizes bilateral stimulation, e.g. activating both sides of your brain by moving eyes side to side, tapping alternating shoulders, or cuing audio in alternating ears. The combination of bilateral stimulation with re-addressing emotionally charged memories helps adapt “yuck” associations toward more calm resolution.

Humanistic

A holistic approach, led by the client’s world view, how they describe their experiences, and what lenses they want to bring to working together. Humanistic means focusing on what it means that you are a whole human, not simply a problem to be solved or a diagnosis to be named. You bring with you an entire history of joys and sorrows, knowledge and questions, both a culmination of experiences and a work in progress.

IFS (Internal Family Systems)

More commonly known as parts work. This modality focuses on identifying different parts of ourselves and how they serve us. As part of this approach, we explore how you conceptualize self, work to liberate parts that feel burdened, and empower those parts of self that you want to better support.

Narrative Therapy

Focusing on what narratives, stories, and frameworks you pull from to help understand and create meaning in your life. In narrative therapy, we engage problem-saturated stories that have made us feel stuck in thinking “This is all that I am.” We move toward identifying and supporting your preferred stories, i.e. ones that allow for new ways of being in and engaging with the world.

Pragmatic

Viewing thought as a problem-solving activity guided by our specific values, commitments, and involvements in a deeply social world. Pragmatism is a melioristic perspective, deeply committed to the belief that things can get better through human effort. Pragmatism sees organism and environment, you and your world, as a mutually adjusting unity. How can we make adjustments in one that better facilitate transacting with the other and vice versa?

Existentialist

Focused on how you create value in your world and how you make meaning through how you choose to project yourself into the world. Existentialism holds in tension the ambiguity of being responsible for choosing how we exist in this world, but alongside the knowledge that this is a world not entirely of our choosing or of our making. We must reckon with how others - both before, alongside, and after us - are similarly entangled in meaning-making.
